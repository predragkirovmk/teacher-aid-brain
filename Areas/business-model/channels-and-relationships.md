# Channels and customer relationships

## Channels — how a headmaster discovers, evaluates and buys

Decided order:

1. **Teacher referral (bottom-up).** A champion teacher runs TeacherAid in their own class
   (free month), colleagues see the students behave, the teacher brings it to the headmaster.
   This is the primary route and the reason pricing is per seat.
2. **Direct outreach to private-school headmasters.** Call, visit, 20 minutes, the report
   sample in hand. The team is in Bitola; Skopje is two hours.
3. **The free live session ("guerilla").** Two people, one real class, one lesson, filmed
   with permission. The school gets a story; the team gets a video and a referral. Run it
   only where a headmaster meeting is already possible.
4. **Education events and teacher social media.** Teacher Facebook groups, the annual
   education conferences, Instagram for the student side (leaderboard clips).
5. **Municipality introductions** (later, public schools). One meeting with the education
   department opens seven schools in Bitola.

Removed from v1 of the canvas: "mobile app" — it is the product, not a channel.

Evaluation happens in the free month; the artefacts that close the sale are the **monthly
report sample** and **usage by the school's own teachers**.

## Customer relationships — how we acquire, support and retain

### Acquire
- Warm: the champion teacher. Cold: the headmaster call. Both end in the same 20-minute
  meeting.

### Onboard
- In-app tutorials, three short videos (teacher first lesson, student first scan, focus
  lock explained), a "first lesson in 10 minutes" checklist.
- No in-person training in the base offer. The champion teacher is the in-school trainer.

### Support
- In-app help and the teacher chatbot as first line; a human (founder, WhatsApp) behind it
  for the first schools. Login at 08:05 is the moment that matters — magic links, no
  passwords to forget.

### Retain
- **Teacher habit.** Daily use is the retention mechanism (decided). Measure: lessons run
  per active teacher per week. Below 2, intervene.
- **The monthly report.** The headmaster's reason to renew arrives every month without
  anyone asking.
- **School-vs-school leaderboard.** Students ask for it; a school that leaves drops off the
  board — a small, real switching cost.
- Renewal conversation: the year in numbers, plus the teachers' own words.

### Signals of trouble
- A school where only the champion is active after month 2.
- Focus flags rising (students revoking the lock) — the game stopped being worth it.
- Anonymous questions at zero — the students do not trust it.
