# Market size — North Macedonia

Scope for the pitch: North Macedonia only (decided). Balkans as the Q&A answer to "too small".

## The numbers (State Statistical Office, start of school year 2024/25)

| | Schools | Students | Teachers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper secondary (гимназија + стручно + уметничко) | **128** | **67,143** | **~7,000** (estimate — see note) |
| Primary + lower secondary | 943 | 180,627 | 19,447 |
| Total | ~1,070 | ~248,000 | ~26,500 |

Note on secondary teachers: the SSO release used for the pitch reports students and schools;
the teacher figure for primary (19,447) is from the same release as reported by the press.
The secondary teacher count is estimated from earlier SSO tables (~7,300–7,500 in the late
2010s) and the reported decade decline of ~5%. **Get the exact 2024/25 figure from the SSO
before the slide is final; say "about seven thousand" on stage.**

Trend: students −20% in secondary over ten years, teachers −5%. Class sizes are falling;
schools are consolidating. Nobody is buying tools for growth — they buy for quality and
reputation. That fits the headmaster pitch.

## Why a small country is the right first market

- One national curriculum (БРО) — one corpus covers every classroom. A US-style district
  patchwork would need hundreds.
- One language for v1. One ministry. One e-register. Seven secondary schools in the team's
  own city.
- Small enough to reach every headmaster in person within a year.
- Judges in Bitola will not believe a global TAM; they will believe "every gymnasium in the
  country by 2028".

## The answer to "and then?"

Every neighbor runs the same shape of system — one ministry, one curriculum, 45-minute
periods: Serbia (~1,800 schools, ~70k teachers), Bosnia and Herzegovina, Albania, Kosovo,
Montenegro. The product changes by language and curriculum corpus, not by design. Roughly
10× the seats within a two-hour flight. (Regional figures are order-of-magnitude — verify
before quoting a specific one.)
