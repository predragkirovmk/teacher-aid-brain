# Customer segments

Three roles in one sale. Confusing them is the most common edtech mistake: the person who
pays is not the person who uses it, and the person who benefits most cannot buy it.

## Buyer — the headmaster

- **Private gymnasium headmaster (beachhead).** Owns the budget. Decides alone or with the
  owner. Sales cycle: one meeting, one week. Cares about enrollment — parents choose private
  schools on reputation, and "AI in every classroom" is a reputation line. Small segment:
  roughly a dozen private secondary schools in the country, mostly in Skopje (verify count).
- **Public secondary school headmaster (second wave).** Does not own the budget in any
  meaningful way; the **municipality** funds the school (block grants, decentralized since
  2005) and procurement above small-value thresholds runs through јавни набавки. The
  headmaster still decides *whether* — the municipality decides *if it can be paid*. Sales
  cycle: months. Bitola has seven public secondary schools (verify list) — the natural first
  public cluster because the team is here and the municipality is one office.
- What they want to be seen as: progressive, in control, ahead of other headmasters. Who
  they want to impress: parents (enrollment), the municipality and МОН (funding, standing),
  other headmasters and local media.
- What they fear: parent complaints about phones, teachers revolting, a tool nobody uses
  after a month, anything they personally have to maintain.

## User — the teacher

- Subject teachers in secondary schools; ~7,000 in the country (estimate — see
  [[market-size]]).
- The **champion**: the teacher who already tries things, uses Kahoot, runs the school
  Instagram. One or two per school. They bring TeacherAid in; they train colleagues by
  example. The bottom-up route is the decided go-to-market.
- The **majority**: 20+ years in, competent, tired of new platforms that add work. Wins
  them only if the first lesson takes less than 10 minutes to set up and the class visibly
  behaves better. Loses them at the first login problem.
- Pain, in their words (still paraphrase — real quotes were never captured, see
  [[risks-and-assumptions]] row 2): planning
  time at night, phones under desks, the same five students answering, no idea what the
  quiet ones did not understand.

## End user — the student

- 15–18, гимназија. Everyone has a phone; most have mobile data. Motivated by peers,
  rank, and not being bored. Will try to game the points — that is fine, it means they care.
- Not a customer. Never pays. But the loudest channel: if students ask for it, the teacher
  keeps it.

## Documented, not in the pitch

- **Parents.** Not a customer, not a user. Must be informed (school's job, template
  provided). Objection to expect: "phones in class". Answer: the teacher decides, the app
  blocks distractions, the school owns the data. See [[data-and-privacy]].
- **МОН.** A possible national-program buyer years out; for now a partner and a source of
  legitimacy. See [[key-partners-resources-activities]].
- **Primary schools.** 943 schools, 19,447 teachers — the bigger market, but younger
  students, fewer phones, and any future phone restriction hits there first. Later.

## Order of attack

**Macedonia first, not city-by-city.** The team is based in Bitola, but the sale is
national from day one — every private gymnasium in the country is a target in year one, not
a later-stage expansion once one city is "done."

Universities are **not** in the order of attack. They are counted in the market sizing and
the product reaches them eventually, but [[feature-spec-v1]] 2 puts them outside v1 and
nothing is sold to a faculty until that changes.

1. The first private gymnasium (warm contact exists — see
   [[startup-weekend-bitola-2026]]), wherever in the country it is, on the strength of the
   free month and the report sample.
2. Two more private schools nationally on the strength of that first monthly report —
   Skopje, Bitola, or anywhere else a headmaster says yes.
3. The first public-school pilot through a municipality, positioned as a story that
   municipality (any municipality) can talk about.
4. Every other public secondary school in the country, sold in parallel — not sequentially
   city by city.
