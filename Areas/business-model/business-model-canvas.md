# Business Model Canvas — TeacherAid (v2)

The corrected canvas. v1 was the handwritten poster from Saturday morning; what changed and
why is at the bottom. Each block links to its detailed note.

## 1. Customer segments — [[customer-segments]]

- **Buyer:** headmasters. Beachhead: **private gymnasiums** (own budget, decide in a week).
  Second wave: public secondary schools, where the **municipality** is the payer.
- **User:** subject teachers in secondary school. First users are the **champion teachers**
  who bring it to the headmaster.
- **End user:** students 15–18, on their own phones.
- Documented, out of the pitch: parents (informed, not customers); МОН as an eventual
  national-program buyer.

## 2. Value propositions — [[value-propositions]]

- **Teacher:** one tool for the whole lesson instead of five. Plan in three sentences.
  Attention handled by the game, not by shouting. Anonymous questions surface what the class
  did not understand.
- **Student:** a class that starts with a question. Safe to be wrong — reasoning earns
  points. Compete with the class next door.
- **Headmaster:** "AI in every classroom" with nothing to learn, install or maintain. A
  monthly one-page report to forward to parents, the municipality and the press.
- **Against alternatives:** built for the Macedonian curriculum and language and the
  45-minute period; the headmaster gets a report, not a login; Curipod/Kahoot/ChatGPT each
  cover one slice and none of them speak to the buyer.

## 3. Channels — [[channels-and-relationships]]

- Teacher referral (bottom-up): a champion teacher runs it, then brings the headmaster.
- Direct outreach to headmasters of private schools (call, visit).
- The **free live session**: the team runs one real class in a school, films it, and the
  school talks.
- Education events, teacher groups on social media.
- Later: municipality introductions for public schools.

## 4. Customer relationships — [[channels-and-relationships]]

- Onboarding: in-app tutorials + short videos; "first lesson in 10 minutes".
- Support: in-app help + the teacher chatbot; a human behind it for the first schools.
- Retention: teacher habit (daily use), the monthly report (the headmaster's reason to
  renew), the school-vs-school leaderboard (students ask for it).

## 5. Revenue streams — [[revenue-and-pricing]]

- **Flat, per school: EUR 199 / month, invoiced yearly (EUR 2,388 / year).** Whole staff
  included — every teacher, not just volunteers. Same price regardless of school size.
  First month free.
- Later: sponsorship (telecom / bank CSR — "AI in Bitola's schools"), grants (EU, UNICEF,
  МОН programs), premium content packs per subject.
- Assumptions needing evidence: willingness to pay EUR 199 flat; whether large schools stay
  profitable at a flat fee (see [[cost-structure]]); renewal rate after the free month.

## 6. Key resources — [[key-partners-resources-activities]]

- The AI lesson engine and prompt library; the **МК curriculum corpus** (БРО programs, then
  teacher-edited lessons — the asset that compounds).
- The live-session engine (QR, points, focus lock).
- The team; an **advising teacher** (missing today — to recruit this week).
- AI credits / provider relationship.

## 7. Key activities — [[key-partners-resources-activities]]

- Build: planner, live session, dashboard, report.
- Curate: curriculum content per subject and year.
- Sell: headmaster meetings, free live sessions.
- Onboard and support teachers.
- Run the leaderboard seasons; produce monthly reports.

## 8. Key partners — [[key-partners-resources-activities]]

- МОН / БРО (endorsement, curriculum data).
- Municipality of Bitola (payer for public schools, door-opener).
- UKLO pedagogy faculty and teacher associations (credibility, research on the opener).
- Telecom (A1 / Telekom: school connectivity, CSR sponsorship).
- AI provider (credits, startup program).
- EDUINO (integration or ally, not a competitor).

## 9. Cost structure — [[cost-structure]]

- **Variable:** AI inference (~5 cents per generated lesson; ~EUR 20 per teacher per year at
  full use), realtime/hosting per session, support time.
- **Fixed:** development, curriculum curation, app-store fees, legal (ДПА, privacy), sales
  time.
- **What gets expensive as it grows:** inference scales with active teachers per school, but
  revenue per school is flat — large schools are the margin risk, not a growth win; sales
  scales with schools; the national leaderboard is nearly free.

## What changed from v1 and why

| v1 (poster) | v2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue streams: empty | Per-seat price, yearly invoice, free month, three secondary streams | Judges score business model; an empty block reads as "no model" |
| Cost structure: empty | AI, hosting, support, dev, sales; what scales | Same |
| Key partners: empty | МОН/БРО, municipality, UKLO, telecom, AI provider, EDUINO | Same, and it answers "who opens the doors?" |
| Channels: "mobile app" | Referral, direct, live session, events | The app is the product, not a channel |
| Key activities: "beginner-friendly, mascot, chatbot" | Build, curate, sell, onboard, run | Those were features; activities are things the company does |
| Key resources: "headmaster" | AI engine, curriculum corpus, session engine, team, advising teacher | The headmaster is a customer |
| Segments: problem/buyer/benefit | Buyer / user / end user, with beachhead and payer named | The municipality as payer for public schools was missing |
| Value props: ego benefits for headmaster | Measurable: the monthly report | "Praise" is not a metric; a report is |
| No answer to "why not ChatGPT + Kahoot" | One tool for the whole lesson; MK curriculum; buyer-side report | The first judge question |
