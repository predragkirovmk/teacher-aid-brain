# Startup Weekend — format and what wins

## This event

- Techstars Startup Weekend Bitola, 11–13 September 2026, House of the Army, Bitola.
- Final pitches Sunday 13 September. **5 minutes pitch + 5 minutes (or more) Q&A.**
- Judges: local founders and investors (as far as the team knows).
- Criteria: **not announced.** Plan for the standard three.
- Slides: Canva, made by the presenter. Ask the organizers for the submission deadline and
  whether presenting from a browser tab is allowed (it is what the demo needs).
- Room: projector and wifi confirmed; live QR possible. Test it (see [[demo-plan]]).

## The standard Techstars criteria

| Criterion | What judges look for | Where TeacherAid scores it |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer validation** | Did you leave the building? Who did you talk to, what did they say, what did you change because of it? | Teacher conversations (log them — [[validation-log]]), the warm headmaster contact, the mentor's reaction. **Weakest today — fix before Sunday.** |
| **Execution and design** | What did you build in 54 hours? Does it work? Does it look like a product? | The live QR demo on the judges' phones ([[prototype]]). |
| **Business model** | Who pays, how much, why, what does it cost, how big is it? | [[revenue-and-pricing]], [[cost-structure]], [[market-size]] — all on one slide. |

Each is typically scored 1–5 and weighted equally. A team that talked to ten customers and
built nothing often beats a team that built everything and talked to nobody.

## What wins, in practice

- A demo the judges *touch* beats a video. (Decided: live QR.)
- One real quote from a customer beats a market-size slide.
- Saying the honest small number beats a fantasy TAM.
- Answering the hard question in Q&A calmly ("yes, Curipod exists, here's why they don't
  sell to headmasters") beats pretending it was not asked.
- Ending on time. Going over is the most common way to lose points for free.

## Q&A behavior

- Repeat the question in five words, answer in three sentences, stop.
- "We don't know yet — here's how we'd find out" is a full-credit answer for a weekend team.
- Never argue with a judge; agree with the premise, then add the fact.
- If a teammate knows the answer better, hand it over by name.
